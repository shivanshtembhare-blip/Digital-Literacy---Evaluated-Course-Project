Case Study: The Colonial Pipeline Ransomware Attack
Ransomware is a type of malicious software designed to block access to a computer system or encrypt its data until a sum of money is paid.

In May 2021, the Colonial Pipeline, which provides 45% of the East Coast’s fuel, fell victim to a devastating attack by the "DarkSide" group (Source: FBI.gov/CISA).

How It Happens: Step-by-Step
The breach typically follows a structured lifecycle:

Initial Access: Attackers gained entry through a compromised Virtual Private Network (VPN) password found on the dark web.

Staging and Discovery: Once inside, the malware moved laterally through the network to identify administrative credentials and sensitive data.

Data Exfiltration: Before encrypting files, the attackers stole nearly 100GB of data to use as "double extortion" leverage.

Encryption: The ransomware was triggered, locking the company’s billing systems and accounting records.

The Ransom Note: A digital message appeared demanding payment in cryptocurrency (approximately $4.4 million) in exchange for a decryption key.

Targets and Consequences
Ransomware groups usually target Critical Infrastructure, healthcare providers, and government agencies because these entities cannot afford downtime.

The consequences for Colonial Pipeline were severe. Fearing the malware could spread to operational technology, the company proactively shut down the pipeline for several days.

This led to widespread fuel shortages, panic buying, and a state of emergency in multiple U.S. states. Even after paying the ransom, the recovery process was slow,

highlighting that a decryption key is never a "magic fix" for restored operations.
